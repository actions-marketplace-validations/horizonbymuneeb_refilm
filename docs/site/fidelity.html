Fidelity levels

refilm checks different things depending on what you asked for. Pick the level before the first capture and say it out loud — it decides what refilm verify treats as a failure, and changing it later means re-recording.

The three levels at a glance. motion is the default.

motion — the default

Use it when the ask is the feel. This is the level most clones actually need, and it is the one that stays useful when the copy, the accent colour and the typeface are yours.

Must match

  • the trigger, and the order events happen in
  • the stagger between siblings
  • the timing of every step, within 80 ms — two frames at 25 fps
  • the easing family (out / in / in-out / linear / spring), with the reference's fitted curve matching the clone within 5% of the travel distance
  • direction and distance of movement, relative to the element's own size, within 10%

May differ

Copy, colours, fonts, images, brand details. Nothing else.

Read the timing columns, not the pixels

For text-heavy sections a motion run will always show a non-zero mean mismatch, because the words are different. That is expected. The verdict is the timing error and the easing family; the mismatch is a trend you use to spot a layout accident.

pixel — when "close" is not the ask

Use it when the words were "match it exactly", "pixel-perfect", "1:1", "the details should match". It fails for reasons that have nothing to do with motion, so order matters:

  1. Fix layout and fonts first. A fallback font changes every frame's pixels, and no amount of easing tuning will recover it.
  2. Then chase timing. Timing errors and pixel errors look similar in a summary and completely different in a sheet.
  3. Then the assets. Same images at the same size, same shaders, same texture resolution.

Adds to motion

  • mean frame mismatch ≤2%
  • no single frame above 5%
  • rest frames ≤0.5%
  • fonts loaded and identical — compare inspect.json › fonts on both captures
  • colours and sizes equal in nodes, the same assets at the same size

Before you judge a pixel run

# the two captures must agree on everything except the clone itself
node -e "const a=require('./ref/capture.json'),b=require('./clone-1/capture.json');
for (const k of ['viewport','fps','trigger','clock','seed','durationMs'])
  if (JSON.stringify(a[k])!==JSON.stringify(b[k])) console.log(k, a[k], '!=', b[k]);"

If that prints anything, the comparison is measuring the setup. Re-record with the same command and change only --url and --out.

mixed — their motion plus your content

Use it for "clone X and add Y": their animation, your pricing tiers, your extra feature row. Keep the additions in their own components or blocks so the cloned region stays measurable, and verify it with --region x,y,w,h when the additions sit next to it.

  • the cloned region passes motion or pixel criteria, on its own
  • the additions are described and shown in the report
  • the additions are verified only for not disturbing the cloned motion — they are not compared against the reference, because there is nothing to compare them to
refilm verify --spec .refilm/runs/hero/spec.json \
  --ref .refilm/runs/hero/ref --clone .refilm/runs/hero/clone-1 \
  --fidelity mixed --region 0,180,1080,420

How the level changes the gate

verify reads the level from --fidelity, or from the spec's own fidelity field when the flag is absent. The level decides which checks run — it does not decide how strictly they are applied.

A skip is not a pass

A check that cannot be measured is reported as skip with the reason, never as pass. A green run that skipped its checks is worse than a red one, because it is a lie with a number attached.

What no level promises

  • The reference's assets are yours to use. Fonts, images, shaders and code from another site still belong to their owners. Cloning the motion is one thing; shipping someone else's font file is another.
  • Physics you approximated. A spring fitted from overshoot and settle time is close, not identical.
  • Randomness you cannot reproduce. Particle systems whose generator you cannot port are compared with a blurred mismatch (compare --blur) and reported as such, next to the sharp number.
  • A level chosen after the fact. Re-labelling a failed pixel run as motion to get a green result is the exact behaviour the tool exists to prevent.
